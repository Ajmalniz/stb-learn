Complete solved exercises from Sindh Textbook Board Chapter 5 — Physical States of Matter. Sections A, B, C, and D (numericals) fully solved for exam preparation.

Section A — Multiple Choice Questions

Tick mark (✓) the correct answer.

1. Which of the following gases diffuses fastest?
A. Hydrogen
B. Chlorine
C. Fluorine
D. Helium
By Graham's law, the lighter the gas the faster it diffuses. Hydrogen (molar mass 2) is the lightest here, so it diffuses fastest.
2. The vapour pressure of a liquid increases with the:
A. Increase of pressure
B. Increase of temperature
C. Increase of intermolecular forces
D. Increase the polarity of molecules
Heating gives molecules more kinetic energy, so more of them escape into the vapour phase. Hence vapour pressure increases with temperature.
3. The freezing point depends upon:
A. Nature of liquid
B. Pressure
C. Temperature
D. Volume
Each substance freezes at its own characteristic temperature, so the freezing point depends mainly on the nature of the liquid (its intermolecular forces).
4. One atmospheric pressure is equal to:
A. 10325 Pascal
B. 106075 Pascal
C. 10523 Pascal
D. 101325 Pascal
1 atm = 760 mmHg = 101325 Pascal (≈ 101.3 kPa).
5. Which of the following does not affect the boiling point?
A. Intermolecular forces
B. External pressure
C. Initial temperature of liquid
D. Nature of liquid
Boiling point depends on intermolecular forces, external pressure, and the nature of the liquid — but not on the liquid's starting temperature.
6. The mobility of liquids is lesser than:
A. Solids
B. Gases
C. Both a & b
D. None of these
Gas molecules move most freely of all. Liquid molecules move less freely than gases (but more than solids), so liquid mobility is less than that of gases.
7. Which of the following has a sharp melting point in solids?
A. Plastic
B. Rubber
C. Glass
D. Diamond
Diamond is a crystalline solid with a regular arrangement, so it has a sharp (definite) melting point. Plastic, rubber, and glass are amorphous and melt over a range.
8. Which of the following is the lightest form of matter?
A. Solid
B. Liquid
C. Gases
D. Plasma
Plasma is the lightest and least dense state of matter — a hot, ionised gas of charged particles (found in stars and lightning).
9. The liquid molecules leave the surface of the liquid in the evaporation process because:
A. Energy is low
B. Energy is moderate
C. Energy is high
D. None of these
Only the surface molecules with high (enough) kinetic energy can overcome the intermolecular forces and escape into the vapour phase during evaporation.
10. The density of gases increases when its:
A. Pressure increased
B. Temperature increased
C. Volume increased
D. None of these
Increasing the pressure squeezes the gas into a smaller volume, so the same mass occupies less space and the density increases. (Raising temperature or volume decreases density.)

Section B — Short Questions

Q1. Define allotropy with examples.

Allotropy: The property by which an element can exist in two or more different physical forms in the same physical state. These different forms are called allotropes and differ in the arrangement of their atoms, giving them different physical properties.

Examples:

  • Carbon: diamond (hard, non-conductor) and graphite (soft, conductor).
  • Sulphur: rhombic sulphur and monoclinic sulphur.
  • Oxygen: dioxygen (O₂) and ozone (O₃).
Q2. What is effusion? Explain with examples.

Effusion is the process in which gas molecules escape one by one through a very small hole (pore) into a region of lower pressure or vacuum, without colliding with each other.

Examples: the slow deflation of a rubber balloon or a car tyre over time, and the leaking of cooking gas from a tiny hole in a cylinder. Lighter gases effuse faster (Graham's law).

Q3. Define the following: Boiling Point, Melting Point, Freezing Point.

Boiling Point: The temperature at which the vapour pressure of a liquid becomes equal to the external (atmospheric) pressure and the liquid changes into vapour throughout. (Water = 100 °C at 1 atm.)

Melting Point: The temperature at which a solid changes into a liquid (solid and liquid exist in equilibrium). (Ice = 0 °C.)

Freezing Point: The temperature at which a liquid changes into a solid. For a pure substance it is the same as its melting point (water freezes at 0 °C).

Q4. What is density? How is the density of a liquid affected by temperature and pressure?

Density is the mass per unit volume of a substance: density = mass ÷ volume (units: g/cm³ or kg/m³).

Effect of temperature: On heating, a liquid expands (volume increases) while its mass stays the same, so its density decreases.

Effect of pressure: Liquids are almost incompressible, so pressure has a very small effect — increasing pressure increases density only very slightly.

Q5. Explain plasma with daily-life examples.

Plasma is the fourth state of matter — a very hot, highly energetic gas whose atoms have been ionised into free electrons and positive ions. As a whole it is electrically neutral and conducts electricity.

Daily-life examples: the glow in neon signs and fluorescent tubes, lightning, the flame of a welding arc, and the Sun and stars (which are made of plasma).

Q6. Justify that the atoms of a Bose–Einstein condensate are "super unexcited" and "super cooled".

A Bose–Einstein condensate (BEC) is formed by cooling a dilute gas of atoms to a temperature extremely close to absolute zero (0 K, −273 °C).

At such an ultra-low temperature the atoms lose almost all their kinetic energy, so they are in their lowest possible energy state — that is why they are called "super unexcited." Because this state is only reached by cooling to nearly absolute zero, the atoms are also "super cooled." All the atoms then behave as a single quantum entity.

Section C — Detailed Questions

Q1. Discuss the property of evaporation in liquids. Which factors affect the evaporation process?

Evaporation is the process by which molecules at the surface of a liquid gain enough kinetic energy to overcome the intermolecular forces and escape into the vapour phase. It occurs at all temperatures (not only at the boiling point) and causes cooling, because the fastest (most energetic) molecules leave, lowering the average energy of those remaining.

Factors affecting evaporation:

  • Temperature: higher temperature → more energetic molecules → faster evaporation.
  • Surface area: larger exposed surface → faster evaporation.
  • Intermolecular forces: weaker forces → the liquid evaporates faster (more volatile).
  • Wind / air movement: moving air removes vapour → faster evaporation.
  • Humidity: higher humidity (more vapour already in air) → slower evaporation.
Q2. Describe Boyle's law with an example.

Boyle's Law: At constant temperature, the volume of a fixed mass of gas is inversely proportional to its pressure.

V ∝ 1/P  (at constant T)  ⇒  PV = constant  ⇒  P₁V₁ = P₂V₂

This means if the pressure is doubled, the volume is halved (and vice versa).

Example: A gas has a volume of 10 dm³ at 1 atm. If the pressure is increased to 2 atm at constant temperature, the new volume = (1 × 10) ÷ 2 = 5 dm³. A squeezed syringe (with the nozzle closed) shows this — pushing the plunger raises the pressure and reduces the gas volume.

Q4. Define and explain Charles's law of gases.

Charles's Law: At constant pressure, the volume of a fixed mass of gas is directly proportional to its absolute (Kelvin) temperature.

V ∝ T  (at constant P)  ⇒  V/T = constant  ⇒  V₁/T₁ = V₂/T₂

Explanation: When a gas is heated, its molecules move faster and strike the walls harder. To keep the pressure constant, the gas must expand, so its volume increases with temperature. Temperature must be taken in Kelvin (K = °C + 273).

Example: A balloon expands when warmed and shrinks when cooled (e.g. put in a fridge).

Q5. Describe the process of diffusion in liquids. State the factors which influence it.

Diffusion in liquids is the spontaneous spreading and mixing of the particles of one liquid into another until they are evenly distributed, due to the random motion of molecules. Example: a drop of ink slowly spreading throughout a glass of water without stirring.

It is slower than diffusion in gases because liquid molecules are closer together and move more slowly.

Factors that influence it:

  • Temperature: higher temperature → faster diffusion.
  • Density / viscosity: more viscous (thicker) liquids diffuse more slowly.
  • Size of molecules: smaller/lighter molecules diffuse faster.
  • Concentration difference: a greater difference speeds up diffusion.
Q6. How is boiling point affected by different factors?
  • External pressure: The boiling point rises when external pressure increases and falls when it decreases. (Water boils below 100 °C on a high mountain because the air pressure is lower; a pressure cooker boils water above 100 °C.)
  • Intermolecular forces / nature of liquid: Liquids with stronger intermolecular forces (e.g. hydrogen bonding) have higher boiling points.
  • Presence of impurities / dissolved solute: Adding a non-volatile solute (like salt) raises the boiling point (elevation of boiling point).
Q7. Define vapour pressure and justify that it is observed in a closed system only.

Vapour pressure is the pressure exerted by the vapour of a liquid when it is in equilibrium with its own liquid at a given temperature.

Why only in a closed system: In a closed container the escaping vapour cannot leave. Molecules keep evaporating while some vapour molecules condense back, until the rate of evaporation equals the rate of condensation — an equilibrium is reached and a steady vapour pressure builds up.

In an open system the vapour continuously escapes into the surroundings, so equilibrium is never established and no measurable vapour pressure develops. Hence vapour pressure is observed only in a closed system.

Section D — Numericals (Solved)

Q1. Convert the following units. (K = °C + 273 ; °C = K − 273)

(A) 100 °C to K:  100 + 273 = 373 K

(b) 150 °C to K:  150 + 273 = 423 K

(c) 780 K to °C:  780 − 273 = 507 °C

(d) 170 K to °C:  170 − 273 = −103 °C

Q2. It is desired to increase the volume of a fixed amount of gas from 90.5 cm³ to 120 cm³ while holding the pressure constant. What would be the final temperature if the initial temperature is 33 °C?

Given: V₁ = 90.5 cm³, V₂ = 120 cm³, T₁ = 33 °C = 33 + 273 = 306 K. Pressure constant → Charles's law.

V₁/T₁ = V₂/T₂  ⇒  T₂ = T₁ × V₂/V₁
T₂ = 306 × (120 ÷ 90.5)
T₂ = 306 × 1.326 = 405.8 K
In °C: 405.8 − 273 = 132.8 °C

T₂ ≈ 405.8 K ≈ 132.8 °C
Q3. A 78 ml sample of gas is heated from 35 °C to 80 °C at constant pressure. What is the final volume?

Given: V₁ = 78 ml, T₁ = 35 + 273 = 308 K, T₂ = 80 + 273 = 353 K. Pressure constant → Charles's law.

V₁/T₁ = V₂/T₂  ⇒  V₂ = V₁ × T₂/T₁
V₂ = 78 × (353 ÷ 308)
V₂ = 78 × 1.146 = 89.4 ml

V₂ ≈ 89.4 ml
Q4. A gas occupies a volume of 40.0 dm³ at standard temperature (0 °C) and pressure (1 atm). When the pressure is increased to 3 atm at unchanged temperature, what is the new volume?

Given: V₁ = 40.0 dm³, P₁ = 1 atm, P₂ = 3 atm. Temperature constant → Boyle's law.

P₁V₁ = P₂V₂  ⇒  V₂ = P₁V₁ / P₂
V₂ = (1 × 40.0) ÷ 3
V₂ = 40.0 ÷ 3 = 13.33 dm³

V₂ ≈ 13.33 dm³
Q5. 800 cm³ of a gas is enclosed in a container under a pressure of 750 mm. If the volume is reduced to 250 cm³, what will be the pressure?

Given: V₁ = 800 cm³, P₁ = 750 mm, V₂ = 250 cm³. Temperature constant → Boyle's law.

P₁V₁ = P₂V₂  ⇒  P₂ = P₁V₁ / V₂
P₂ = (750 × 800) ÷ 250
P₂ = 600000 ÷ 250 = 2400 mm

P₂ = 2400 mm
Q6. The pressure of a sample gas is 8 atm and the volume is 15 litres. If the pressure is reduced to 6 atm, what is the volume?

Given: P₁ = 8 atm, V₁ = 15 L, P₂ = 6 atm. Temperature constant → Boyle's law.

P₁V₁ = P₂V₂  ⇒  V₂ = P₁V₁ / P₂
V₂ = (8 × 15) ÷ 6
V₂ = 120 ÷ 6 = 20 L

V₂ = 20 litres
