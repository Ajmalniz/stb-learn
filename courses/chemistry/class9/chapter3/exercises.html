Section A — Multiple Choice Questions

Tick mark (✓) the correct answer.

1. In 1869, Mendeleev put forward his periodic law based on:
A. Atomic Number
B. Chemical properties
C. Physical properties
D. Atomic Mass
Mendeleev arranged elements in order of increasing atomic mass and stated that the properties of elements are a periodic function of their atomic masses.
2. The periodic table is divided into s, p, d, and f blocks based on:
A. Atomic Radius
B. Electronic Configuration
C. Ionization Energy
D. Electron Affinity
Each block is named after the subshell (s, p, d, or f) into which the last electron enters, i.e. the element's electronic configuration.
3. The 4th and 5th periods in the periodic table are known as:
A. Short period
B. Long period
C. Normal period
D. Very long period
The 4th and 5th periods each contain 18 elements, so they are called long periods.
4. Which one of the following decreases along the period?
A. Ionization Energy
B. Atomic Radius
C. Electronegativity
D. Electron Affinity
Across a period the nuclear charge increases and pulls the electrons closer, so the atomic radius decreases. Ionization energy, electronegativity, and electron affinity all increase.
5. The elements of the VIIA group are known as:
A. Lanthanides
B. Actinides
C. Halogens
D. Noble Gases
Group VIIA (group 17) — F, Cl, Br, I, At — are called the halogens ("salt formers"). They each have 7 valence electrons.
6. According to Moseley, the chemical properties of elements are a periodic function of their:
A. Atomic Size
B. Atomic Mass
C. Atomic Radius
D. Atomic Number
Moseley showed that properties depend on the atomic number (number of protons), not the atomic mass. This is the basis of the modern periodic law.
7. The shielding effect across a period:
A. Increases
B. Decreases
C. Moderate
D. Remains the same
Across a period the number of inner shells stays the same, so the shielding effect remains almost constant even though the nuclear charge increases.
8. The ability to attract a shared pair of electrons is called:
A. Electron Affinity
B. Electronegativity
C. Ionization Energy
D. Shielding Effect
Electronegativity is the tendency of an atom to attract the shared (bonding) pair of electrons towards itself in a covalent bond. Fluorine is the most electronegative element.
9. In a group, electron affinity values decrease from top to bottom because:
A. Atomic size is normal
B. Atomic size increases
C. Atomic size decreases
D. Atomic size is same
Going down a group the atomic size increases, so the nucleus attracts an incoming electron less strongly. Hence less energy is released and electron affinity decreases.
10. All transition elements are:
A. Gases
B. Metals
C. Non-metals
D. Metalloids
All transition elements (the d-block) are metals — hard, shiny, good conductors of heat and electricity, with high melting points.

Section B — Short Questions

Period Group

Q2. Describe the trend of electronegativity within a group and a period.

Across a period (left → right): Electronegativity increases. The nuclear charge increases and atomic size decreases, so the atom attracts the shared electrons more strongly. Example: Na < Mg < Al < Si < P < S < Cl.

Down a group (top → bottom): Electronegativity decreases. The atomic size increases and shielding increases, so the nucleus attracts shared electrons less. Example: F > Cl > Br > I.

Fluorine is the most electronegative element (4.0 on the Pauling scale).

Q3. Explain the similarity of chemical and physical properties of elements in a family (group).

Elements of the same family (group) have the same number of valence (outermost) electrons. Because chemical behaviour is decided by valence electrons, all members react in a similar way and form similar compounds.

Example — Group IA (alkali metals): Li, Na, K each have 1 valence electron. They are all soft, reactive metals; they all lose 1 electron to form +1 ions and react vigorously with water to give a base and hydrogen gas.

Their physical properties (density, melting point, atomic size) are similar and change in a regular, gradual way down the group.

Q4. Justify that the periodicity of properties is dependent upon the number of protons in an atom.

The number of protons is the atomic number. In a neutral atom, number of protons = number of electrons, which fixes the electronic configuration and therefore the number of valence electrons.

As the number of protons increases one by one, the electronic configuration repeats a similar outer-shell pattern at regular intervals (e.g. every element with 1 valence electron behaves like an alkali metal).

Since properties depend on valence electrons, and the valence pattern recurs periodically with increasing proton number, the properties also recur periodically. This is exactly what Moseley's modern periodic law states — properties are a periodic function of atomic number (protons). ✔

Going down the group, molecular size and intermolecular forces increase, so the physical state changes gas → liquid → solid.

Q6. Why do alkaline earth metals show irregular melting and boiling points?

The alkaline earth metals (Group IIA: Be, Mg, Ca, Sr, Ba) do not all have the same type of crystal (metallic) structure — they pack their atoms differently (e.g. hexagonal, face-centred, body-centred).

Because the arrangement of atoms and the strength of metallic bonding do not change in a regular way down the group, their melting and boiling points do not follow a smooth trend — they rise and fall irregularly.

Q7. Why do ionization energy, electron affinity, and electronegativity exhibit the same trend in periods and groups?

All three quantities arise from the same cause — the strength of attraction between the nucleus and the outer/added electrons, which depends on nuclear charge, atomic size, and shielding.

Across a period: nuclear charge increases and atomic size decreases (shielding constant) → the pull on electrons increases → all three increase.

Down a group: atomic size increases and shielding increases → the pull on outer electrons decreases → all three decrease.

Since they respond to the same factors in the same way, they follow the same trend.

Section C — Detailed Questions

Q1. Discuss in detail the long form of the periodic table.

The long form of the periodic table is based on the modern periodic law: "The properties of elements are a periodic function of their atomic numbers." Elements are arranged in order of increasing atomic number.

Periods (horizontal rows) — there are 7:

  • Period 1 → 2 elements (H, He)
  • Periods 2 & 3 → 8 elements each (short periods)
  • Periods 4 & 5 → 18 elements each (long periods)
  • Period 6 → 32 elements; Period 7 → incomplete
  • The period number = number of occupied shells.

Groups (vertical columns) — there are 18: Elements in a group have the same number of valence electrons and similar properties (e.g. Group IA alkali metals, Group VIIA halogens, Group VIIIA noble gases).

Blocks: The table is divided into s, p, d, and f blocks according to the subshell being filled.

Arrangement: Metals are on the left, non-metals on the right, metalloids lie along the staircase line, and the transition metals (d-block) sit in the middle. The lanthanides and actinides (f-block) are placed in two rows at the bottom to keep the table compact.

Q5. Discuss that Mendeleev's periodic law provided a base for the modern periodic table.

Mendeleev's contribution (1869):

  • He arranged the known elements in order of increasing atomic mass and grouped elements with similar properties in vertical columns.
  • He introduced the framework of periods and groups still used today.
  • He left gaps for undiscovered elements and predicted their properties (eka-aluminium = Gallium, eka-silicon = Germanium). When these elements were later found, their properties matched his predictions — proving the value of his table.

How the modern table built on it:

  • The modern periodic table keeps Mendeleev's group–period structure.
  • Moseley replaced atomic mass with atomic number, which removed the anomalies in Mendeleev's table (e.g. the Ar–K and Co–Ni order).

Because the modern table is essentially Mendeleev's design corrected and extended, his periodic law is rightly said to be the base of the modern periodic table.

Q6. Explain how the shielding effect influences periodic trends.

Shielding effect: The inner-shell electrons repel the outer electrons and partly "screen" them from the full pull of the nucleus. So the outer electrons feel a reduced effective nuclear charge instead of the full nuclear charge.

Down a group: The number of inner shells increases, so shielding increases. The outer electrons are held less tightly → atomic size increases, while ionization energy, electron affinity, and electronegativity all decrease.

Across a period: The number of inner shells (shielding) stays almost the same while nuclear charge increases, so the effective nuclear charge increases. Hence atomic size decreases, while ionization energy, electron affinity, and electronegativity all increase.

Therefore the shielding effect is one of the key factors that controls all the periodic trends.
